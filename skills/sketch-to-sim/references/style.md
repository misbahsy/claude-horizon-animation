# The look, and the bar to check against

## The reference

The paper trebuchet demo: a pencil sketch on an A1 sheet lying on a honey-oak desk, soft window light falling across it in broad diagonal bands, a pencil and a blue-sleeved eraser left near the top edge. READ writes orange handwritten labels one part at a time while that part's strokes glow orange, then cleans the drawing to ink and adds pencil dimension lines. LIFT stands the ink drawing up as white paper cut-outs with black outlines, casting shadows, gives them depth, and fills them with wood. Runs are shot from a low three-quarter hero angle with the wall behind; notes are handwritten in dark ink; measured values sit in small white mono tags. The replay is a hard cut to a lower, closer camera with a darker vignette and a 0.25× badge. The UI is a warm off-white card, a chapter bar along the bottom, and a right-hand panel for the sandbox.

## Quality bar

Check stills of every chapter at full size and the finished MP4 at normal speed.

**The chain is real.** Every stage of the story happens in the physics, at the values shown. Speeds in notes are measured.

**The sketch reads.** Lines are dark enough to see at 1080p, the labels do not collide with the drawing or each other, and each label sits by the part it names.

**The lift lands on the drawing.** The flat cut-outs at the start of LIFT cover the ink lines exactly; if they do not, a part's profile and its sketch disagree.

**Nothing leaves the frame that matters.** In run chapters the whole chain is in shot, including the far end of any swing. In the sandbox the machine sits left of the panel.

**Notes are placed.** Each note is near what it describes, readable against what is behind it, and on screen for at least a second.

**The failure is legible.** Someone watching once can see where and why the first run stops, and the tweak visibly fixes that.

**The sound matches.** Clacks, knocks and the bell land on the contacts that make them; nothing buzzes while at rest.

**Timing.** A film of 70 to 90 seconds; READ under 15 s; the replay covers the one moment that matters, not the whole run.
